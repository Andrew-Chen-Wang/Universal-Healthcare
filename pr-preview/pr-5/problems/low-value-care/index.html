Problem · Waste, fraud & low-value care

Low-value care and overtreatment

Tests and treatments with little benefit (imaging for routine back pain, some screenings, end-of-life interventions patients don't want) cost an estimated $76–101B a year.

Evidence

  • Overtreatment or low-value care $76–101B a year.
    Sources: Shrank, Rogstad & Parekh, JAMA (2019)

If nothing changes

Low-value care is falling only slowly: the share of traditional Medicare beneficiaries who received at least one of the low-value services studied fell from 36.3% in 2014 to 33.6% in 2018.

Sources: JAMA Network Open (Mafi et al.) (2021)
Compare approaches, from keeping current law to the largest changes

Who's affected

Who is hurt
  • People with ongoing or specialist care needs: Low-benefit tests and treatments expose them to harm.
  • Health insurers, brokers, and their shareholders: They pay for tests and treatments with little benefit.
  • Taxpayers and public budgets: They pay for tests and treatments with little benefit.
Who gains while it lasts
  • Hospitals, clinics, and care organizations: Per-service payments reward additional low-value care.
  • Drug and device makers: They sell devices and tests used in low-value care.

      • Send planned surgery to high-volume centers
